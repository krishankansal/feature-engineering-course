What is a Polynomial Feature?¶

Definition: A Polynomial Feature is created by taking powers of an existing feature.

The Hidden Pattern¶

Look at the marks:

Hours Hours² Marks
1 1 10
2 4 40
3 9 90
4 16 160

Notice:

$$ \text{Marks} = \text{Hours}^2 \times 10 $$

The relationship is actually based on Hours².

But the model cannot see Hours² unless we provide it.

Understanding the Pattern¶

Consider the values:

1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5

The increase is constant:

+1 +1 +1 +1

Now suppose we create a new feature by squaring each value.

Then:

1² → 2² → 3² → 4² → 5²

which gives:

1 → 4 → 9 → 16 → 25

Now look at the increases:

+3 +5 +7 +9

So the original feature increases at a constant rate:¶

1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5

+1 +1 +1 +1

while the squared feature increases at an increasing rate:¶

1 → 4 → 9 → 16 → 25

+3 +5 +7 +9

This is the key intuition:

Squaring transforms the original feature and introduces a non-linear pattern into its values.

1. Before Creating a Polynomial Feature¶

In the first graph, we consider:

Hours Studied → Marks

As the number of hours increases, the marks also increase. However, the increase is not constant.

The relationship forms a curve rather than a straight line.

This indicates that the relationship between the feature and the target is non-linear.

2. Creating a Polynomial Feature¶

To capture this non-linear relationship, we transform the original feature.

For example:

Hours Studied → Hours²

So, instead of using only:

Hours Studied

we create an additional feature:

Hours Squared

This is called a Polynomial Feature.

3. After Creating the Polynomial Feature¶

The second graph shows the relationship between:

Hours² → Marks

The relationship now appears much closer to a straight-line relationship.

This means that the transformed feature can represent the original non-linear relationship in a form that a linear model can handle more effectively.

4. The Main Concept¶

The important point is:

Polynomial feature engineering does not change the target values. It transforms the input features to help the model capture non-linear relationships.

Conceptually:

Original Feature
      ↓
  Transformation
      ↓
Polynomial Feature
      ↓
Better representation of
the non-linear relationship

Interaction Features¶

Advertising and Sales¶

Suppose a company spends money on:

  • TV Advertising
  • Social Media Advertising

and we observe the resulting sales.

Imagine this:

TV Advertising Social Media Advertising Sales
10 2 50
10 8 70
50 2 70
50 8 130

Now look carefully at what is happening.

First Comparison¶

Keep TV advertising fixed at 10:

TV = 10

Social Media = 2  → Sales = 50
Social Media = 8  → Sales = 70

Increasing social-media advertising increases sales.

Fine.

Now look at the same change when TV advertising is high:

TV = 50

Social Media = 2  → Sales = 70
Social Media = 8  → Sales = 130

The increase is much larger.

The Interesting Observation¶

The effect of Social Media advertising appears to depend on the level of TV advertising.

In other words:

Social Media advertising does not operate completely independently of TV advertising.

And this is exactly the situation where an interaction can be useful.

We can create:

$$ TV \times SocialMedia $$

Let's calculate it.

TV Social Media TV × Social Media
10 2 20
10 8 80
50 2 100
50 8 400

Now we have created a new feature:

TV_Social_Interaction Why Does This Make Sense?

Think about the last two cases.

Case 1¶

TV = 50

Social Media = 2

50 × 2 = 100

Case 2¶

TV = 50

Social Media = 8

50 × 8 = 400

The interaction value becomes much larger when both advertising channels are high.

So the new feature represents something that the individual columns don't explicitly represent:

The combined level of TV advertising and Social Media advertising.
